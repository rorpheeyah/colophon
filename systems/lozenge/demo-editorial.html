Transport · Punctuality

Four minutes is doing a great deal of work

The network reports 88.6% of services on time. Change the window by ninety seconds and two of its four lines change places.

By the transport desk 17 September 4,730 services examined

A punctuality figure is a threshold wearing the clothes of a measurement. The network publishes one number for the year, and the number is true; what it does not carry is how much of its own weight rests on where the threshold was drawn.

Across 4 lines and 4,730 scheduled services, 4,190 arrived inside the published window. That is the headline. Beneath it, Ridge returns 94.1% and Kiln returns 73.6%, a spread of 20.5 points between the best and worst performing line on the same network in the same year.

The threshold is the argument

Every line concentrates its late arrivals in a narrow band on either side of the window rather than spreading them evenly, which is what you would expect from a timetable written to be met rather than to be accurate. Move the boundary and you do not shave a little off each line; you reclassify a cluster.

A timetable written to be met is not the same document as a timetable written to be accurate, and only one of them can be audited.

From the method note

Kiln is the clearest case. It runs the fewest services of the four and carries 9 halts, and its margin is spent before the last two. The line does not fail at a point; it fails gradually and then reports the failure at the end.

What the return does not say

The figures below are the filed return, unadjusted. They are what the regulator holds and what the operator stands behind, and they are the reason the spread above can be stated at all — a published threshold is at least a threshold anyone can move.
